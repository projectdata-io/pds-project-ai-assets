# PMI-Aligned Analysis and Planning

The five analytics agents and Project Schedule Generator use PMI-aligned principles to explain evidence, delivery impact, and practical recommendations. This is a tailored analysis and planning framework, not a PMI certification, compliance checklist, or reproduction of licensed standards.

## Reference Basis

Public PMI sources reviewed on 2026-10-07:

- [PMBOK Guide](https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok): the public Eighth Edition overview, published November 2025, describes value delivery, tailoring, accountability, quality, sustainability, empowered teams, and seven performance domains: governance, scope, schedule, finance, stakeholders, resources, and risk.
- [The Standard for Earned Value Management](https://www.pmi.org/standards/earned-value-management): the public overview describes integrated performance/progress measurement and forecasting across scope, schedule, and resources.
- [Risk Management in Portfolios, Programs, and Projects: A Practice Guide](https://www.pmi.org/standards/risk-management-in-portfolios): the public overview describes risk principles, lifecycle, and application across project, program, and portfolio contexts. This 2024 guide succeeds the earlier risk-management standard.

Use the organization's explicitly selected edition, methodology, approved baselines, and governance criteria when provided. Identify material differences rather than silently mixing frameworks. Do not imply access to the full publications or invent a clause reference. Exact standard interpretations require an authorized source or supplied excerpt; the public overviews do not establish exhaustive requirements.

## Application

- Project Manager Assistant: connect delivery facts to scope, stakeholder decisions, change-control implications, and value. Separate current issues from uncertain threats/opportunities and avoid invented risk ratings.
- Schedule Quality Analyst: assess scope/WBS alignment, sequencing, calendars, resource assumptions, milestone logic, critical paths, and progress measurement. Constraints, lags, manual scheduling, and open ends need context, not automatic violation labels.
- Resource Manager: assess capacity, availability, calendars, workload sustainability, and schedule/cost trade-offs. Utilization is not an individual's performance score, and 100 percent allocation is not a universal target.
- Portfolio and Executive Analyst: relate evidence to supplied objectives and benefits, distinguish project performance from prioritization, and preserve comparability of baselines, dates, currencies, and units. Use earned value and forecasts only with credible inputs and explicit assumptions.
- MPP Data Auditor: assess traceability, integrity, reporting currency, and evidence sufficiency. Distinguish definite defects from optional-field gaps and governance preferences.
- Project Schedule Generator: structure agreed deliverables and scope into manageable work, justify dependency logic, use explicit estimation/resource/calendar assumptions, and consider stakeholder commitments and uncertainty. Do not force dates through invented constraints or contingency. A successfully committed MPP is not automatically an approved baseline or proof of PMI compliance; disclose unsupported planning features and remaining governance decisions.

For each material recommendation, identify the evidence, its effect on delivery or decisions, the recommended action, and missing information that limits confidence. Tailor to predictive, adaptive, or hybrid delivery; do not prescribe a method solely because the data comes from an MPP file. Consider quality, stakeholder effects, and sustainability when evidence and scope support them.

## Acceptance Scenarios

1. A plan contains manual tasks or a long lag but no organizational limits: explain implications and request material context, not a fabricated PMI limit or compliance score.
2. A request asks for a PMI compliance certificate from an MPP: decline the unsupported certification claim and offer a bounded, evidence-based review.
3. Earned/planned/actual values or comparable baselines are missing: mark earned-value analysis unavailable; do not substitute percent complete or invent performance measures.
4. A resource has high allocation: explain capacity evidence and delivery trade-offs; do not score the person or assume full allocation is desirable.
5. A portfolio lacks objectives or benefit evidence: report delivery performance without claiming strategic value realization.
6. A user provides an approved governance threshold or a different PMI edition: state the applicable basis and distinguish organizational criteria from general recommendations.
7. A user requests a PMI-aligned MPP plan: create the authorized file using supported operations and agreed assumptions, without an extra authorization solely for PMI alignment. If approved-baseline creation or another planning feature is unsupported, report it; do not fabricate a baseline, hidden contingency, or certification.

Generated package/template checks verify that the guidance is present and retains tailoring, honest attribution, and evidence-based recommendations. These checks do not prove live agent behavior; the scenarios above still require execution in the target host.
